I have a secret, and I hope you can't figure it out because it is quite embarrassing: 
```V hfr NV gb uryc zr jevgr zl Arbivz cyhtvaf```

To encrypt that secret message, I used a nearly unbreakable cypher strategy
called the Caesar Cipher. Since the Caesar Cipher is prolific in the cyber
security industry, it is generally taught to first year students in a
cryptography class.

I know that you, dear reader, respect my privacy and would
never decode my secret message, so I will let you in on a little secret: `g?`
works in visual mode to perform a Caesar Cipher on your current selection (alternatively `g??` for an entire line), and
since the Caesar Cipher is so efficient you can just use it again on the same chunk
of text to decode it.

As mentioned in _Fun with g: Vol1_ it is common to assign a mnemonic to `g` commands to assist in remembering them. `g?` can simply be remember as a command not to remember. The `?` is for why would you ever need this.
